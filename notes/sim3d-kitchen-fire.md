# Notes: never pour water on a grease fire (sim3d kitchen_fire, 2026-10-25)
| claim | checked value | source |
|---|---|---|
| water sinks in oil | water denser than cooking oil (~1.00 vs ~0.92 g/cm3), sinks to the bottom, turns to steam | Cal Poly Housing (water sinks, turns to steam, splatters oil and spreads fire); densities general physics |
| oil temp | cooking-oil flash point typically ~190 C (375 F); auto-ignition of veg/canola oil 406-424 C | Cal Poly Housing; Univ. of Maryland DRUM "Auto-Ignition of Cooking Oils" (Buda-Ortins 2011) |
| steam expansion | water -> steam volume x1,700 at standard temperature and pressure | Wikipedia "Steam" |
| fireball to ceiling | commonly shown in fire-dept demos; phrased as "can reach the ceiling" | general fire-safety demos (not a precise number) |
| what to do | turn off heat, cover with metal lid/baking sheet/fire blanket, leave covered ~30 min; baking soda for small fires; no flour or sugar (combustible); if spreading, evacuate and call fire department | Cal Poly Housing |
Simplifications: "a tiny splash can erupt" is the standard fire-service warning; sizes are illustrative.
